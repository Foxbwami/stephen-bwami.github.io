STEPHEN BWAMI — SAMPLE DATASET GUIDE
====================================

Dataset: Sample retail orders (synthetic demonstration data)
Currency: Kenyan shillings (KES)
Period: 14–20 October 2026
Files:
- sample-before.csv: raw example with deliberate quality issues
- sample-after.csv: cleaned version after documented transformations

WHAT IS INCLUDED
Each row represents one retail order. The order_id identifies the order.
The dataset is synthetic and contains no real customer information.

ISSUES DEMONSTRATED IN THE RAW FILE
1. One repeated order record (ORD-1003).
2. Leading/trailing spaces in customer names and numeric values.
3. Inconsistent county and payment-status capitalisation.
4. Dates represented in multiple formats.
5. A comma-formatted amount that needs numeric parsing.
6. Missing unit price and total for ORD-1008.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE CLEANED FILE
- Removed the confirmed repeated ORD-1003 row.
- Trimmed unnecessary whitespace.
- Standardised county and payment-status labels.
- Converted dates to YYYY-MM-DD.
- Stored numeric values without thousands separators.
- Kept the missing values for ORD-1008 blank.

IMPORTANT ASSUMPTIONS
The repeated ORD-1003 row is treated as an accidental duplicate for this
demonstration. In a real project, confirm the business key and source records
before deleting any repeated row.

The missing price and total for ORD-1008 were NOT replaced with zero.
A blank means unknown or unavailable; zero would mean a confirmed zero value.
The record is retained so that the missing information remains visible.

SUGGESTED EXERCISE
1. Download both CSV files.
2. Compare row counts and inspect the repeated order.
3. Standardise the date, county and payment-status fields.
4. Check numeric types and identify missing values.
5. Explain why the missing values were preserved.
6. Write a short cleaning log describing each transformation.

You can use Excel, Google Sheets, Python/pandas, R or another CSV tool.
The sample is for learning and portfolio demonstration, not business reporting.
